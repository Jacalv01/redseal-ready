# Mild Steel Electrodes

The electrode is the consumable at the centre of SMAW — it determines the mechanical properties of the weld, what positions you can weld in, what polarity you need, how the arc feels, and how you clean up afterward. Picking the wrong electrode for the job produces a weld that either fails inspection or requires expensive repair. This lesson gives you the knowledge to select correctly every time.

---

## AWS electrode classification system — how to read an electrode label

All SMAW electrodes for mild and low-alloy steel are classified per AWS A5.1.[^1] The classification appears on the electrode coating and the box — learn to read it.

### The E-XXXX system

**Format: E-XX(XX)-X**

| Position | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **E** | Electrode (as opposed to R = rod for GTAW filler) | E in E7018 |
| **1st and 2nd digits** | Minimum tensile strength of deposited weld metal in ksi (thousands of psi) | 70 in E7018 = 70 ksi (70,000 psi) minimum tensile strength |
| **3rd digit** | Welding position(s) | 1 = all positions; 2 = flat and horizontal only; 4 = flat, horizontal, vertical-down |
| **4th digit (last)** | Coating type + polarity + current type | 8 in E7018 = low-hydrogen potassium coating, DCEP or AC |

**Example — E7018:**
- E: electrode
- 70: 70,000 psi (480 MPa) minimum tensile strength of weld metal
- 1: all positions (flat, horizontal, vertical-up, overhead)
- 8: low-hydrogen potassium coating; DCEP or AC[^1]

**Example — E6010:**
- E: electrode
- 60: 60,000 psi (415 MPa) minimum tensile
- 1: all positions
- 0: high-cellulose sodium coating; DCEP only[^1]

### CSA classification (Canadian parallel)

CSA W48 classifies SMAW electrodes using a parallel format. The CSA designation for E7018 is **E4918** (CSA uses metric-based strength: 49 = 490 MPa minimum tensile strength).[^4] [^5]

The AIT curriculum uses CSA designations alongside AWS — on a Canadian job site you will see both on the same box.

| AWS | CSA | Tensile strength |
|---|---|---|
| E6010 | E4310 | 60 ksi / 415 MPa |
| E6011 | E4311 | 60 ksi / 415 MPa |
| E6013 | E4313 | 60 ksi / 415 MPa |
| E7014 | E4814 | 70 ksi / 480 MPa |
| E7018 | E4918 | 70 ksi / 480 MPa |
| E7024 | E4824 | 70 ksi / 480 MPa |

## Electrode coating types — the 4th digit explained

The last digit of the AWS classification is the most important for your day-to-day work — it tells you what the coating is, what polarity to use, and what current type is acceptable.[^1]

| Last digit | Coating type | Current/polarity | Key characteristic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | High-cellulose sodium | DCEP only | Deep penetration; stiff arc; E6010 |
| **1** | High-cellulose potassium | DCEP or AC | Similar to -0 but AC-compatible; E6011 |
| **2** | High-titania sodium | DCEN or AC | Soft arc; easy slag; limited penetration |
| **3** | High-titania potassium | DCEP, DCEN, or AC | Very easy to run; E6013 |
| **4** | Iron powder titania | DCEP, DCEN, or AC | Higher deposition; gravity feed; E7014 |
| **5** | Low-hydrogen sodium | DCEP only | Hydrogen-controlled weld metal |
| **6** | Low-hydrogen potassium | DCEP or AC | Hydrogen-controlled, AC compatible |
| **7** | Iron powder iron oxide | DCEP, DCEN, or AC | High iron powder content |
| **8** | Low-hydrogen potassium + iron powder | DCEP or AC | The standard structural electrode; E7018 |

## The six electrodes every welder must know

These are the electrodes you'll encounter on the job and on the Red Seal exam. Know all details for each.[^1] [^2]

### E6010 (CSA: E4310)

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Coating** | High-cellulose sodium |
| **Polarity** | DCEP ONLY — will not work on AC or DCEN |
| **Positions** | All positions (1 = flat, horizontal, vertical-up, overhead) |
| **Penetration** | Deep — the highest penetration of the common electrodes |
| **Arc character** | Stiff, digging arc — forceful |
| **Slag** | Thin, fast-freezing slag — easy to remove, re-strikes easily |
| **Typical amperage (1/8" / 3.2 mm)** | 75–125 A (check manufacturer table)[^2] |
| **Typical amperage (3/32" / 2.4 mm)** | 50–90 A[^2] |
| **Applications** | Root passes on pipe, cross-country pipeline welding, open-root groove welds, bridge fabrication |
| **Primary limitation** | Not low-hydrogen — cannot be used on high-strength or high-restraint joints without specific engineering approval |

**Why E6010 is special:** The stiff, fast-freezing arc that E6010 produces is what makes it the choice for open-root pipe welds. The welder can "keyhole" the root (maintain a small keyhole of molten metal at the root opening) and control the root bead profile precisely. No other common SMAW electrode does this as well.[^2]

---

### E6011 (CSA: E4311)

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Coating** | High-cellulose potassium |
| **Polarity** | DCEP or **AC** |
| **Positions** | All positions |
| **Penetration** | Deep (similar to E6010) |
| **Arc character** | Similar to E6010 but softer — AC compatible |
| **Slag** | Thin, fast-freezing |
| **Typical amperage (1/8" / 3.2 mm)** | 75–125 A[^2] |
| **Applications** | Same as E6010 where AC power is the only option, field repairs, contaminated surfaces, arc blow situations |
| **Key advantage over E6010** | Runs on AC — used when DC is unavailable or when arc blow is a problem |

---

### E6013 (CSA: E4313)

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Coating** | High-titania potassium |
| **Polarity** | DCEP, DCEN, or AC |
| **Positions** | All positions |
| **Penetration** | Shallow/medium — lower penetration than E6010/11 |
| **Arc character** | Very soft, stable, easy to control — ideal for beginners |
| **Slag** | Thick, easily removed |
| **Typical amperage (1/8" / 3.2 mm)** | 80–130 A[^2] |
| **Applications** | Light sheet metal, thin plate, auto body repair, maintenance welding where penetration must be controlled |
| **Primary limitation** | Not low-hydrogen; low penetration makes it unsuitable for thick structural work |

**Why E6013 is the "training electrode":** The soft arc and gentle penetration make it easy for beginners to run a flat bead. But it's genuinely the wrong electrode for structural work — don't let easy-to-run fool you into thinking it's a structural electrode.[^2]

---

### E7014 (CSA: E4814)

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Coating** | Iron powder titania |
| **Polarity** | DCEP, DCEN, or AC |
| **Positions** | All positions |
| **Penetration** | Medium |
| **Arc character** | Smooth, easy to run; high deposition rate |
| **Slag** | Heavy; self-releasing in some cases |
| **Typical amperage (1/8" / 3.2 mm)** | 100–150 A[^2] |
| **Applications** | General fabrication, fillet welds, flat and horizontal butt welds; where higher deposition than E6013 is needed |
| **Key feature** | Iron powder in the coating boosts deposition efficiency compared to E6013 |

---

### E7018 (CSA: E4918) — THE structural electrode

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Coating** | Low-hydrogen potassium + iron powder |
| **Polarity** | DCEP or AC |
| **Positions** | All positions |
| **Penetration** | Medium |
| **Arc character** | Smooth, medium-stiff; weld pool quite fluid |
| **Slag** | Heavy, glassy, full-coverage; must be chipped completely between passes |
| **Typical amperage (1/8" / 3.2 mm)** | 100–165 A[^2] |
| **Typical amperage (3/32" / 2.4 mm)** | 70–110 A[^2] |
| **Applications** | Structural steel (CSA W59, AWS D1.1 prequalified), pressure vessels, code-quality welds, all-position fillet and groove welds on medium and thick plate |
| **Critical requirement** | LOW HYDROGEN — must be stored in a heated oven (120–150°C) and used within exposure time limits to prevent moisture absorption |

**E7018 is the workhorse of structural welding in Canada.** If you work in structural steel, bridges, pressure vessels, or code-governed fabrication, you will run more E7018 than any other electrode.[^1] [^2]

---

### E7024 (CSA: E4824)

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Coating** | Iron powder iron oxide (heavy iron powder) |
| **Polarity** | DCEP, DCEN, or AC |
| **Positions** | **Flat and horizontal ONLY** (4th digit = 2) |
| **Penetration** | Medium–shallow |
| **Arc character** | Very smooth; runs very hot; can drag on the work |
| **Slag** | Very heavy iron powder slag; gravity-fed deposition |
| **Typical amperage (5/32" / 4.0 mm)** | 140–200 A[^2] |
| **Applications** | High-deposition flat and horizontal fillet and butt welds; production welding where speed matters |
| **Key limitation** | Position-restricted — cannot be used vertical or overhead |

**E7024 is a production electrode.** The heavy iron powder content gives extremely high deposition rates in flat/horizontal position — makes economic sense on long horizontal seams. Not a general-purpose electrode.[^2]

## Coating functions — what the coating actually does

The electrode coating is not just a holder — it performs multiple essential functions simultaneously:[^1] [^3]

1. **Shielding:** decomposes in the arc to produce CO₂, CO, and other gases that displace air (O₂, N₂) from the arc zone — prevents oxidation and nitriding of the weld metal

2. **Slag formation:** the non-gas components of the coating melt and form a slag layer over the weld pool — the slag protects the cooling weld from atmosphere, shapes the bead profile, and slows the cooling rate

3. **Arc stabilization:** ionizable elements in the coating (potassium compounds in particular) help sustain the arc, especially for AC applications where the arc must re-ignite every half-cycle

4. **Deoxidation:** fluxing agents in the coating combine with and remove oxygen from the weld pool — manganese and silicon deoxidizers are common

5. **Alloying:** some coatings contain alloying elements (manganese, silicon) that transfer into the weld metal to achieve the specified mechanical properties

6. **Hydrogen control (low-H coatings):** low-hydrogen coatings are specifically formulated to exclude moisture and minimize hydrogen content in the weld metal — critical for crack prevention

---

## Functions of the slag

The slag layer does more than sit on top of the weld bead:[^1] [^3]

- **Atmospheric protection during solidification:** while the weld is still molten, the slag blanket prevents oxidation that would otherwise occur at 1,500°C+
- **Cooling rate control:** the slag acts as an insulating blanket, slowing the cooling rate slightly — this is beneficial for some microstructures
- **Bead shape control:** the surface tension and viscosity of the slag help shape the weld bead profile (especially in vertical and overhead welding)
- **Flux function:** some slag components react with the molten weld metal to refine it (remove sulfur, phosphorus, and oxides)

**Slag removal is mandatory between passes.** Slag inclusions (trapped slag between passes) are a weld defect that reduces ductility and strength. Use a chipping hammer and wire brush to completely remove slag before every subsequent pass.[^1]

## Care, handling, and storage

### Low-hydrogen electrode storage — CRITICAL

E7018 and other low-hydrogen electrodes (suffix -5, -6, -8) are **hygroscopic** — they absorb moisture from the atmosphere. Moisture absorbed into the coating diffuses as hydrogen into the weld metal, causing cold cracking (hydrogen-assisted cracking — HAC).[^1] [^2]

**Storage requirements for low-hydrogen electrodes:**

| Condition | Temperature | Time limit |
|---|---|---|
| Unopened hermetically sealed cans | Room temperature — do not open until needed | Indefinite (per seal integrity) |
| Holding oven (after opening) | **120–150°C (248–302°F)** | Indefinite while in oven |
| Atmospheric exposure (out of oven) | Room temperature | **4 hours maximum** before reconditioning required[^2] |
| Recondition (re-dry) temperature | 260–430°C (per AWS A5.1 electrode category) | Per manufacturer — typically 1 hour minimum |

**Practical rule:** If an E7018 electrode has been sitting out more than 4 hours, re-dry it before use. If you don't know how long it's been out, re-dry it.

### General electrode handling

- Keep electrodes dry — moisture ruins all electrode types, not just low-hydrogen
- Store in a dry location — a damp toolbox is the enemy of electrode shelf life
- Inspect before use: coating should be intact, undamaged, free of heavy rust
- Discard electrodes with cracked, chipped, or severely damaged coatings — damaged coating produces porosity and arc instability
- Never use the bare end to strike the arc (the un-coated stub end) — no shielding

## What the textbook doesn't tell you

**E6010 is dying in some shops and absolutely dominant in others.** In Alberta pipeline and field erection work, E6010 is the foundational skill — it's what you root pipe with and what you cap with in the field. In a structural fab shop that runs everything on AC transformers, you'll never see E6010 because it requires DCEP. Know the process before you criticize an electrode choice.[^2]

**The 4-hour exposure rule for E7018 is a starting point, not a guarantee.** In high-humidity environments (coastal, rainy day, humid summer), electrodes absorb moisture faster. Some WPS specifications for critical welds (pressure vessels, bridges) specify 2 hours or even 1 hour exposure limits. The WPS governs — if it says 2 hours, 2 hours it is.[^1]

**"Drag" technique on E7018.** One technique for running E7018 in the flat position is to literally drag the coating on the base metal — keeping the electrode almost touching the work. The coating thickness sets the arc length. This sounds wrong but it works because the coating is thicker than the arc gap needed. It produces very consistent arc length for beginners. Don't use this technique vertical or overhead.[^3]

**Never use E7018 on rusty or painted surfaces.** E7018's low-hydrogen advantage is useless if the base metal is contaminated — rust and paint introduce hydrogen directly into the weld pool regardless of the electrode's hydrogen level. Clean the base metal first. Always.[^2]

## Key terms

- **AWS A5.1:** the AWS specification governing classification, testing, and requirements for carbon steel SMAW electrodes
- **Low-hydrogen electrode:** an electrode with a coating specifically formulated to minimize diffusible hydrogen in the deposited weld metal — critical for crack prevention in structural and pressure work
- **High-cellulose electrode:** coating containing a high percentage of organic cellulosic materials — burns to produce CO₂/CO shielding gas; deep penetration; fast-freezing slag (E6010, E6011)
- **High-titania electrode:** coating with titanium dioxide as the primary ingredient — smooth arc, heavy slag, easy slag removal, lower penetration (E6013)
- **Iron powder electrode:** coating contains iron powder that melts into the weld, increasing deposition efficiency (E7014, E7018, E7024)
- **Hygroscopic:** the property of absorbing moisture from the atmosphere — E7018 and all low-hydrogen electrodes are hygroscopic
- **Deposition efficiency:** the percentage of electrode weight converted into deposited weld metal (remainder is slag, spatter, stub ends)
- **Slag:** the molten flux layer floating on the weld pool — solidifies as a glass-like covering over the bead that must be removed before the next pass
- **Reconditioning (re-drying):** heating absorbed-moisture electrodes to specified temperatures to drive out moisture and restore low-hydrogen classification

## Common exam trap

- **"E6013 is the best electrode for structural work because it's easy to use"** — false and dangerous. E6013 is easy to use but has low penetration and is not low-hydrogen. It is not acceptable for code structural work under CSA W59 or AWS D1.1.
- **"E7018 can run on DCEN"** — false. E7018 runs on DCEP or AC. DCEN is not listed for E7018 in AWS A5.1. Running E7018 on DCEN produces poor arc characteristics and potentially non-conforming weld mechanical properties.
- **"The first two digits of the classification are the tensile strength in MPa"** — false. The first two digits are tensile strength in ksi (thousands of psi). 70 ksi ≠ 70 MPa. (70 ksi ≈ 483 MPa). The CSA system uses MPa (490 MPa → CSA prefix 49, e.g., E4918).
- **"E6010 can run on AC if you push the amperage higher"** — false. E6010 requires DCEP due to the chemistry of its high-cellulose sodium coating. It will not sustain a stable arc on AC regardless of amperage.
- **"Slag inclusions are acceptable in a multi-pass weld"** — false. Slag inclusions between passes are a defect. Each pass requires complete slag removal before the next pass. Slag trapped in the weld is a volumetric discontinuity that can become a defect if it exceeds code limits.

## Practice question preview

**Q:** An E7018 electrode has been sitting in an open box on the shop floor for 6 hours during an 8-hour shift. Before the welder uses it for a structural steel weld governed by CSA W59, what must be done?

A) The electrode is fine — 6 hours is within the 8-hour workday limit  
B) The electrode must be reconditioned (re-dried) in an oven before use  
C) Lightly sandpaper the coating to remove surface moisture  
D) Switch to E6010 instead — it is not affected by moisture  

**Correct: B**

**Explanation:** E7018 is a low-hydrogen electrode that can absorb moisture from the atmosphere. The maximum atmospheric exposure time per AWS A5.1 and most manufacturers' recommendations is 4 hours before reconditioning is required. This electrode has been exposed for 6 hours — it must be reconditioned (re-dried at 260–430°C for 1+ hour, per manufacturer specifications) before use on a code weld. Option A is wrong — 6 hours exceeds the 4-hour limit. Option C (sandpapering) does not drive out absorbed moisture from the coating bulk. Option D is wrong — E6010 is also affected by moisture (though it is not classified as low-hydrogen, wet E6010 still causes porosity; also, E6010 would not necessarily be permitted by the WPS).
