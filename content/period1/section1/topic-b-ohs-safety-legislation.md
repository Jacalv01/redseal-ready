# Safety, Legislation, Regulations and Industry Policy

## The three-layer OHS system in Alberta

Every welder in Alberta works under a stacked set of rules. From highest authority down:[^1]

1. **OHS Act** — the law itself. Passed by the Alberta Legislature. High-level: defines duties, worker rights, enforcement powers.
2. **OHS Regulation** — supporting rules under the Act. Covers administrative stuff (reporting, records, discipline).
3. **OHS Code** — the technical rules. **This is the one you'll actually reference on the job.** Contains specific numerical requirements: fall protection thresholds, PPE requirements, ventilation, welding-specific parts.

**When someone says "check the Code," they mean the OHS Code — not the Act.**

## Who is legally responsible on a worksite

Alberta OHS law assigns duties to five roles. All five can be prosecuted for violations.[^2]

| Role | Their obligation |
|---|---|
| **Employer** | Ensure the health and safety of all workers on their sites; provide equipment, training, supervision |
| **Prime contractor** | (on multi-employer sites) Coordinate OHS across all employers on that site |
| **Supervisor** | Ensure workers under their direction follow safe work practices and know the hazards |
| **Worker** | Take reasonable care to protect yourself and others; report unsafe conditions; use required PPE |
| **Suppliers / self-employed** | Ensure equipment/materials they supply meet OHS requirements |

**A journeyperson mentoring an apprentice is a supervisor under the Act.** That's why they can (and will) shut down your work if you're doing something unsafe.

## The three basic worker rights

Memorize these. They come up on every safety-related exam question:[^3]

1. **Right to know** — about hazards in your workplace and how to protect yourself (this is the whole basis of WHMIS)
2. **Right to participate** — in identifying and resolving safety concerns (through joint health and safety committees, safety reps, or directly)
3. **Right to refuse** dangerous work — you can refuse work you have reasonable grounds to believe is dangerous to you or others, **without penalty**

The right to refuse has a formal process (report to supervisor → investigation → if unresolved, OHS officer). You can't just walk off site — you must follow the process to be protected.

## WHMIS 2015 — what you actually need to know

WHMIS (Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System) is the Canadian implementation of the UN's Globally Harmonized System (GHS). It's federally-mandated but enforced provincially through OHS.[^4]

**As a welder, you'll encounter WHMIS in three places:**

1. **Product labels** — shielding gas cylinders, cleaning solvents, degreasers, penetrant testing chemicals, paint/primers. Look for:
   - Product name
   - Pictogram (see below)
   - Signal word ("Danger" or "Warning")
   - Hazard statement + precautionary statement
   - Supplier identifier

2. **Safety Data Sheets (SDS)** — 16-section document your employer must make accessible for every hazardous product on site. The sections you'll actually use as a welder:
   - Section 2: Hazard identification
   - Section 4: First-aid measures
   - Section 7: Handling and storage
   - Section 8: Exposure controls / PPE

3. **Worker education** — your employer must train you before you handle a product, and re-train annually.

### The 9 WHMIS 2015 pictograms

You will be tested on these. Common ones you'll see in a welding shop:

| Pictogram | What it means | Where you'll see it |
|---|---|---|
| **Flame** | Flammable | Acetylene, propane, solvents |
| **Flame over circle** | Oxidizer | Oxygen cylinders |
| **Gas cylinder** | Compressed gas | Every shielding gas + fuel gas cylinder |
| **Exploding bomb** | Explosive / self-reactive | Some pyrophoric fluxes |
| **Corrosion** | Skin/metal corrosive | Battery acid, some pickling pastes |
| **Skull and crossbones** | Acutely toxic (fatal) | Some cleaning agents |
| **Health hazard** | Carcinogen / respiratory sensitizer | Welding fumes (hexavalent chromium — SS welding), manganese fume |
| **Exclamation mark** | Skin/eye irritant, less severe | Many degreasers |
| **Environment** | Aquatic toxicity | Waste solvents |

## PPE — the welding minimum

Alberta OHS Code Part 18 (Personal Protective Equipment) sets the baseline. For welding, industry-standard minimums:[^5]

- **Eye/face:** welding helmet with correct shade filter lens (varies by process/amperage — Shade 10-13 for arc; see [OHS Code Part 18 s.230] and CSA Z94.3 for exact selection)
- **Hands:** welding gloves rated for the process (leather gauntlets for stick, thinner for TIG)
- **Body:** flame-resistant (FR) clothing, leathers for heavy work, no synthetics
- **Feet:** CSA-approved Grade 1 (green triangle) safety boots
- **Hearing:** protection when noise > 85 dBA
- **Respiratory:** required for confined space, galvanized/coated materials, stainless (Cr(VI)), or when ventilation is inadequate — see SDS for the specific consumable

**The apprentice is responsible for PPE inspection before every shift.** Cracked helmet lens, contaminated gloves, worn boots — you flag and replace before starting work.

## Hazard assessment

Alberta OHS Code Part 2 requires **written hazard assessments** for the work being done. As a welder, before you strike an arc, you (or your crew) should have identified:[^6]

- **Fire hazards** — flammables within 35 ft/10 m (per NFPA 51B / CSA W117.2)
- **Confined space** — is the work area a permit-required confined space?
- **Fall hazards** — working at heights ≥ 3 m triggers fall protection requirements (OHS Code Part 9)
- **Fume/gas hazards** — ventilation adequate? Respirator required?
- **Electrical hazards** — grounding, cable condition, wet floors
- **Pinch/crush hazards** — from material handling
- **Overhead hazards** — cranes, hot work above others

Formal name: **FLHA** (Field Level Hazard Assessment). Most industrial sites require one before every task change or new work area.

## Key terms

- **OHS Act / Regulation / Code:** the three-tier legal framework governing workplace safety in Alberta
- **Prime contractor:** the legally-designated coordinator of OHS on multi-employer sites
- **WHMIS 2015:** Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System — Canadian GHS-aligned hazard communication
- **SDS:** Safety Data Sheet — 16-section product hazard document (replaces the old MSDS)
- **PPE:** Personal Protective Equipment
- **FLHA:** Field Level Hazard Assessment — on-site pre-task hazard review
- **Right to refuse:** worker's legal right to refuse dangerous work, following the formal process

## Common exam trap

Test writers love to blur these:
- **Act vs Regulation vs Code** — they are three different documents. The Code has the technical numbers.
- **WHMIS 1988 vs WHMIS 2015** — WHMIS 2015 uses GHS pictograms; the old 1988 symbols are obsolete but sometimes appear as wrong-answer distractors.
- **MSDS vs SDS** — MSDS is the old name (pre-2015); SDS is current. If you see "MSDS," it's either a stale document or a distractor.
- **Worker rights** — always three: **know, participate, refuse**. Distractors may add "right to compensation" or "right to training" (training is an *employer duty*, not a worker right).
- **Fire watch distance** — the widely-cited "35 feet / 10 m" comes from NFPA 51B and CSA W117.2, not the OHS Code directly. If a question asks under which document, cite the standard, not the Code.
