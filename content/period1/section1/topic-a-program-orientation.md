# Welder Apprenticeship Program Orientation

## What you're signing up for

Becoming a Journeyperson Welder in Alberta means you complete **three periods of apprenticeship training**, then pass the **Alberta Journeyperson exam** — and if you want to work anywhere in Canada, you challenge the **Interprovincial (Red Seal) exam** on top of that.[^1]

Each period is:
- **1,560 hours** of paid, on-the-job work with a certified journeyperson mentor
- **8 weeks** of classroom instruction (240 hours) at a NAIT, SAIT, Keyano, Lakeland, Portage, Red Deer Polytechnic, Northwestern Polytechnic, Olds, Medicine Hat, Lethbridge Polytechnic, Northern Lakes, or Alberta Pipe Trades College campus[^2]
- A **period exam** written at the end of classroom instruction — **you need 70% to pass and move on**[^3]

Total to journeyperson: **~4,680 on-the-job hours + 720 classroom hours + 3 period exams**.

## The three parties in your apprenticeship contract

Apprenticeship isn't just you and your employer. It's a legal agreement between **three parties**:[^4]

| Party | Their job |
|---|---|
| **You (the apprentice)** | Show up, do the work, log your hours, complete assignments, pass exams |
| **Your sponsor/mentor** (employer + journeyperson) | Give you real welding work across the required competencies, verify your hours in your competency portfolio, sign off on your progress |
| **Apprenticeship and Industry Training (AIT)** | Register your contract, deliver period exams, issue your credential |

If any one of these three drops the ball, your apprenticeship stalls. That's why your **competency portfolio** matters — it's your proof that all three parties did their part.

## Your competency portfolio (the record book)

The AIT-issued record book tracks two things:[^5]

1. **On-the-job hours** — you must complete a minimum **1,560 hours per period** (some are logged, some verified by your mentor)
2. **Core competences** — each period has ~12 core competences (e.g., "Foundational Skills, Safety and Procedures", "SMAW", "GMAW/FCAW/MCAW/SAW"). You need your mentor to sign off that you're competent in **at least 9 of 12** each period.

**If you're missing hours OR missing competency sign-offs, you cannot write your period exam.** Track both. Weekly.

## Pay progression (why finishing matters)

Alberta apprentices are paid a percentage of the lowest-paid journeyperson at their company:[^6]

- **Period 1:** at least **60%** of journeyperson rate
- **Period 2:** at least **75%**
- **Period 3:** at least **90%**
- **Journeyperson:** 100% + eligibility for foreman/supervisor roles, Red Seal mobility across Canada

Skipping the paperwork or bombing a period exam directly costs you 15-40% of your paycheque compared to a peer who moves through cleanly.

## Where welders work in Alberta

The curriculum prepares you for three main employment sectors:[^7]

- **Industrial** — oil & gas, pipelines, refineries, petrochemical plants, mining. Highest pay, most travel, often camp work. B-pressure ticket is a big earner here.
- **Construction** — structural steel, bridges, high-rise, commercial buildings. Ties to CSA W47.1 / W59 code work.
- **Manufacturing / fabrication shops** — pressure vessels, tanks, custom fab, repair shops. Indoor, stable hours, often lower pay but predictable.

Your Red Seal endorsement lets you cross provincial lines without re-qualifying.[^8]

## External organizations you should know

These groups shape the trade — you'll see their names on every code, procedure, and welder qualification card in Canada:[^9]

| Organization | What they do |
|---|---|
| **AIT (Apprenticeship and Industry Training)** | Alberta trade regulator — sets curriculum, issues credentials |
| **CCDA (Canadian Council of Directors of Apprenticeship)** | Runs the Red Seal program nationally |
| **CWB Group (Canadian Welding Bureau)** | Certifies welders and welding companies to CSA standards |
| **CSA Group** | Publishes welding codes (W47.1, W59, B51) |
| **AWS (American Welding Society)** | Publishes electrode classifications (E7018, ER70S-6, etc.) used across North America |

You don't need to memorize what each one does yet — but recognize the names. They come up constantly.

## Key terms

- **Apprentice:** a person legally contracted to learn a trade under a journeyperson mentor
- **Journeyperson:** a certified tradesperson who has completed all three periods, on-the-job hours, and the AIT journeyperson exam
- **Red Seal:** national endorsement (Interprovincial) letting you work in any province without re-testing
- **AIT:** Alberta's Apprenticeship and Industry Training branch
- **Core competence:** a skill area (e.g., SMAW) your mentor must verify you can perform
- **Competency portfolio / Record book:** the AIT-issued log tracking your hours and competencies

## Common exam trap

Multiple-choice questions on this topic love to swap:
- **AIT vs CWB vs CSA** — these are different organizations. AIT regulates apprenticeship in Alberta; CSA writes codes; CWB certifies to those codes.
- **Period hours vs total hours** — 1,560 is *per period* on-the-job. Total OTJ to journeyperson = 4,680.
- **Pass mark for period exam** — it's **70%**, not 60% and not 65%.
