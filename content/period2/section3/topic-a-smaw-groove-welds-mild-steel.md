# SMAW Groove Welds on Mild Steel

Period 2 SMAW is where you stop practising positions and start making welds that will be cut up, bent, and broken in a test. Groove welds are the basis of structural connections, pressure vessels, and pipeline joints — every application that needs full penetration. The 1G/2G/3G combination with E6010 root and E7018 fill is the cornerstone SMAW groove qualification used by CWB, AWS, and ASME codes across Canada. Getting this right opens the door to certified welder status.

---

## The weld procedure: why E6010 root and E7018 fill/cap?

This combination is not arbitrary — each electrode is chosen for what it does best.[^1][^4]

### E6010 — the root pass electrode

**Why it's used for roots:**
- **Fast-freeze** — the slag solidifies almost immediately behind the arc, giving the welder manual control over puddle drainage through the root
- **Highly penetrating arc** — the high-cellulose coating produces a stiff, forceful arc that drives through tight root openings
- **Keyhole technique** — with E6010, you can deliberately punch through the root and maintain a controlled keyhole that fuses both sides of the joint simultaneously
- **DC+ only** (DCEP) — gives the penetrating, stiff arc characteristics

**What it can't do:** E6010 produces a rough, irregularly-textured weld surface. It has moderate strength (~60 000 psi/415 MPa) and moderate toughness. Multipass groove welds built entirely with E6010 are possible but unusual on structural code work where E7018 is almost always used for fill and cap.[^1]

**CSA designation:** E4310 (the "43" = 430 MPa tensile, "1" = all-position, "0" = DC only)[^4]

### E7018 — the fill and cap electrode

**Why it's used for fill and cap:**
- **Low hydrogen** — the iron-powder, low-hydrogen coating produces < 8 mL H₂/100 g of deposited weld metal, preventing hydrogen-induced cracking (HIC) in the HAZ
- **Higher strength** — 70 000 psi (480 MPa) minimum tensile
- **Excellent toughness** — Charpy V-notch impact values at -20°C and -30°C meet structural code requirements
- **Smooth finish** — makes an excellent cap pass for visual and NDT inspection
- **AC or DCEP** — flexibility for different power sources

**CSA designation:** E4918 (430 MPa tensile, all-position, iron-powder low-hydrogen)[^4]

## Joint preparation for groove welds

Groove geometry affects penetration, weld quality, and efficiency.[^2][^3]

### Single V-groove (most common for up to ~20 mm plate)

**Standard dimensions (CSA W59 / AWS D1.1 prequalified):**[^2][^3]
- **Bevel angle:** 22.5° per side (45° included angle for tight groove) OR 30° per side (60° included angle — most common)
- **Root opening:** 1.5–3 mm (typically 2.4 mm / 3/32")
- **Root face (land):** 1.5–2.5 mm — prevents burn-through on root pass
- **Included angle:** 60° is most common for SMAW with E6010 root

**Root face purpose:** The small flat at the root of the bevel controls burn-through during the root pass. Too thin → blow-through; too thick → incomplete penetration.

### Double V-groove (for plate > ~20 mm, welded from both sides)

- Reduces total weld metal volume by ~50% vs single V
- Reduces angular distortion (balanced passes both sides)
- Requires back-gouging and back-welding or careful through-penetration from one side

### Backing bar (with E7018, 1GF/2G/3GF positions)

A **backing bar** is a steel strip placed behind the root opening, touching both joint faces. Its purpose is to catch and support the root pass weld metal, allowing full penetration without blow-through.[^1]

**Backing bar dimensions (typical):** 5 mm × 25 mm, tack-welded to one side before fit-up
**Run-off tabs:** Additional small steel plates at each end of the joint — allows the weld to run out past the end of the joint, preventing craters at the joint ends

**When using backing bar (1GF, 2G, 3GF with E7018 only):**
- No keyhole technique needed — the backing catches the root
- Larger root opening (3–6 mm) is acceptable with backing
- E7018 can be used straight through without E6010 root — simpler, all-low-hydrogen

## Root pass technique: E6010 keyhole method (no backing bar)

This is the most skill-intensive weld in the Period 2 curriculum.[^1][^4]

**Setup:**
- Root opening: 2.4 mm (3/32") — consistent is critical
- Root face: 1.5–2 mm
- Tack welds every 50–75 mm; tacks must have full fusion at root
- Grind any rough tack starts/stops

**Welding technique:**
1. Strike arc on the beginning of the joint
2. Establish a small puddle; begin to see the keyhole (small elliptical hole) at the leading edge of the puddle
3. **Maintain the keyhole:** if the keyhole disappears → slow down (increase heat dwell); if the keyhole grows → speed up or increase arc length slightly
4. **Motion:** A slight side-to-side or circular motion keeps fusion at both root faces. Avoid wide weaving — keep it to 1-2 electrode diameters
5. **At stops/starts:** stop well before the end of a rod, grind the stop to a tapered end, restart 10 mm back on the solidified weld, then advance to the joint again (prevents cold lap at restart)
6. **Travel angle:** 5-15° push (forehand) for root on groove weld — different from FCAW drag technique

**Root pass quality indicators:**
- Consistent backside bead width (1.5–3 mm wider than root opening)
- No high-low (uneven fusion on each root face)
- No burn-through holes
- Visible fusion on backside of plate (back bead showing)

## Fill passes: E7018

After the root pass is complete and cleaned:[^1]

**Cleaning between passes:**
- Wire brush to remove all surface slag
- Chip any heavy slag pockets
- Grind any rough spots, spatter within 25 mm of the joint, or arc strikes
- Arc strikes outside the weld zone must be reported and ground smooth — they are considered defects under AWS D1.1 and CSA W59

**E7018 fill technique:**
- **Stringer beads:** preferred for most fill passes — better control, more predictable fusion, less distortion vs weave
- **Slight weaving acceptable** on the cap pass only for width control (don't exceed 3× electrode diameter weave width)[^4]
- **Travel angle:** 10-15° drag (backhand) — E7018 is a drag process unlike E6010 root
- **Amperage:** 115-165 A for 4 mm (5/32") E7018 flat; reduce 10-15% for vertical up

**Interpass temperature:**
- **Maximum interpass temperature for mild steel:** generally 250°C — verify with your WPS[^3]
- **Minimum interpass temperature = preheat temperature** — don't let the joint cool below preheat between passes
- Measure with a contact thermometer or temperature-indicating crayon (Tempilstik) at least 75 mm from the weld[^3]

## Positions: 1G, 2G, 3G

### 1G — Flat position
- Plate is horizontal; welding downhand into the joint
- Easiest position for groove welds; gravity pulls the puddle into the joint
- Root pass: consistent travel speed, watch keyhole
- Fill/cap: stringer beads, minimal weave on cap

### 2G — Horizontal position
- Plate is vertical; weld groove is horizontal
- Root pass: more challenging — puddle wants to droop downward
- Technique: **upward gun/electrode angle** (point electrode slightly upward, 10-15° above horizontal) to counteract droop
- Fill passes: **offset stringer technique** — each pass placed slightly below the previous toe, building up from bottom to top
- Cap pass: wash bead technique — slight upward motion to cover

### 3G — Vertical position (up)
- Plate is vertical; weld progresses upward
- **Root pass (E6010):** 
  - Reduce amperage 10-15% from flat setting
  - Triangular weave or J-motion — pause at each toe (1-2 seconds) to allow fusion before moving
  - Keyhole may pulse open/closed — normal; adjust by modulating travel speed
- **Fill passes (E7018):**
  - Triangular weave: move up the centre, pause at each toe
  - Do NOT pause in the centre — that's where undercut forms
  - Stringer beads also acceptable but slower for wider grooves

## Low-hydrogen practices for E7018

E7018 low-hydrogen coating must be protected from moisture absorption. Moisture → H₂ → HIC.[^1][^4]

**Storage rules:**
- Store unopened cans at room temperature and humidity — do not allow to get wet
- Open cans: hold in a **heated rod oven at 120–150°C (250–300°F)**[^1]
- If E7018 electrodes are left out of the oven for > 4 hours: **re-dry at 300–350°C (570–660°F) for 1 hour minimum**[^1]
- Maximum of 2 re-dry cycles — after that, discard
- E6010 electrodes: cellulosic — do NOT re-dry in oven (removes needed moisture from coating)

**Signs of moisture in E7018:**
- Porosity in the weld (hydrogen gas escaping as pores)
- Rough, bubbly surface
- Hydrogen cracking (delayed cracking — can appear 24–48 hours after welding in high-carbon or restrained joints)

## Welding qualifications (CWB / CSA W47.1)

Understanding what position qualifications cover is critical for job readiness.[^3][^4]

**CWB welder qualification test positions:**

| Test position | AWS designation | What it qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| Flat plate groove | 1G | Flat only |
| Horizontal plate groove | 2G | Flat + horizontal |
| Vertical plate groove | 3G | Flat + horizontal + vertical |
| Overhead plate groove | 4G | Flat + overhead |
| **3G + 4G** | Combined | Flat + horizontal + vertical + overhead (all position plate) |

**Pipe qualification (adds pipe positions):**
- 2G pipe = fixed pipe, horizontal weld axis → adds flat + horizontal
- 5G pipe = fixed pipe, vertical axis → adds vertical + overhead
- 6G pipe = 45° fixed = most demanding → qualifies all positions on plate and pipe

**For Period 2:** the focus is 1G, 2G, 3G on plate. Pipe qualification is Period 3 territory.

## Numbers you need to memorize

- **E6010 polarity:** DCEP only[^1]
- **E7018 polarity:** DCEP or AC[^1]
- **E6010 AWS designation:** E6010 (60 000 psi tensile, all-position, DC only)[^1]
- **E7018 AWS designation:** E7018 (70 000 psi tensile, all-position, low-hydrogen iron powder)[^1]
- **E7018 oven holding temperature:** 120–150°C (250–300°F)[^1]
- **E7018 re-dry temperature:** 300–350°C (570–660°F) for 1 hour[^1]
- **E7018 out-of-oven time limit:** 4 hours maximum before re-dry[^1]
- **Standard V-groove included angle (SMAW):** 60° (2 × 30° per side)[^2][^3]
- **Root opening (SMAW E6010 root):** 2.4 mm (3/32")[^1]
- **Root face (land):** 1.5–2 mm[^1]
- **Maximum interpass temperature (mild steel):** typically 250°C — check WPS[^3]
- **Vertical up amperage reduction vs flat:** 10–15%[^1]
- **3G + 4G plate qualification covers:** all positions on plate[^4]

## What the textbook doesn't tell you

**The keyhole is not a defect — it's the technique.** A common mistake is seeing the root opening burn through and trying to "heal" it by slowing to a stop. If you stop with a keyhole open, you get a hole in the root. Instead, speed up or increase arc length slightly to let the puddle close the keyhole as you move forward. The E6010 root technique is dynamic — you're managing the keyhole, not eliminating it.

**Electrode angle changes completely between E6010 root and E7018 fill.** E6010 root: push/forehand (5-15° tilt with arc leading the puddle). E7018 fill: drag/backhand (10-15° tilt with arc trailing). Welders who habitually push their E7018 fill passes trap slag — slag inclusions are almost guaranteed.

**Check interpass temperature before each pass, not just between pairs.** A joint that starts hot (from the previous pass) can exceed 250°C by the third or fourth pass on a cold day without active cooling. Use a Tempilstik. On a hot summer day in an Alberta shop without AC, the joint stays hot longer — wait for the temperature to drop if needed.

**The backing bar affects the weld more than you think.** A backing bar that isn't fully tacked will pull away from the root opening during welding — leaving a gap that the weld metal can't bridge cleanly. Every tack on the backing bar needs to be solid. Some shops use permanent backing (stays in place); some call for removable backing (removed after welding, root side ground smooth).

## Key terms

- **E6010:** cellulosic high-penetration electrode; fast-freeze; DCEP only; used for root passes in open groove welds
- **E7018:** iron-powder low-hydrogen electrode; excellent toughness and strength; DCEP or AC; used for fill and cap passes
- **Keyhole:** controlled opening ahead of the molten puddle in a groove root pass; indicates through-penetration
- **Root pass:** first weld pass in a groove joint; establishes full penetration; most critical pass in terms of technique
- **Fill pass:** subsequent passes deposited over the root to build up the groove cross-section
- **Cap pass:** final pass covering the filled groove; becomes the visual inspection surface
- **Root opening:** the gap between the root faces of the joint members (2.4 mm typical for SMAW E6010)
- **Root face (land):** the small flat at the root of the bevel that controls burn-through (1.5–2 mm)
- **Backing bar:** steel strip placed behind the root opening to support the root pass and allow full penetration
- **Interpass temperature:** temperature of the weld joint between passes; maximum typically 250°C for mild steel
- **Low hydrogen electrode:** electrode with coating that produces <8 mL H₂/100 g weld metal; prevents HIC
- **HIC (Hydrogen-Induced Cracking):** delayed weld cracking driven by diffusible hydrogen, residual stress, and susceptible microstructure
- **Prequalified WPS:** Welding Procedure Specification meeting code-defined geometry and conditions that doesn't require testing
- **1G/2G/3G/4G:** plate groove weld positions — flat/horizontal/vertical/overhead

## Common exam trap

- **E6010 is forehand (push) for root; E7018 is backhand (drag) for fill** — reversing these is a common exam trap. Exam will describe one electrode and ask travel angle.
- **E7018 must be kept in a heated rod oven at 120–150°C** — NOT in the original sealed container (that's for storage only, not open packages). NOT at ambient temperature. NOT in a warmbox designed for E6010.
- **E6010 should NOT be re-dried in an oven** — it's a cellulosic electrode; the coating intentionally contains some moisture. Oven drying ruins E6010. Exam may offer "re-dry all electrodes before use" as a correct choice.
- **Backing bar allows E7018 root pass** — if backing is present, you don't need E6010. You can run E7018 straight from root to cap. Exam may imply E6010 is always required.
- **3G (vertical up) requires REDUCED amperage vs flat** — about 10–15% less. Distractor answer often says "increase amperage for more penetration."
- **4G overhead + 3G vertical qualifies ALL plate positions** — but NOT pipe positions. Pipe positions require separate pipe qualification tests.

## Practice question preview

**Q:** A welder completes the root pass of a 2G (horizontal position) single V-groove weld using E6010. The next pass will be a fill pass with E7018. What is the CORRECT travel angle for the E7018 fill pass?

A) 5–15° push (forehand), arc leading the puddle
B) 10–15° drag (backhand), arc trailing the puddle
C) 90° perpendicular to the plate surface
D) 30–45° push (forehand) for better penetration

**Correct: B**

**Explanation:** E7018 is a drag (backhand) electrode for fill and cap passes — the electrode should be angled 10–15° toward the direction of travel with the arc trailing the puddle. This prevents slag from being pushed ahead of the puddle and trapped under the weld metal (slag inclusions). (A) 5–15° push is the correct technique for E6010 root passes — switching to E7018 and maintaining the same push angle is a common cause of slag inclusions. (C) Perpendicular provides no angular direction and gives no control advantage. (D) 30–45° push would force slag ahead of the puddle even more aggressively — guaranteed slag inclusions.
