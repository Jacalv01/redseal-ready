# GMAW, FCAW and MCAW on Mild Steel Pipe

Pipe welding pays. Structural welders on plate top out around $45/hr in Alberta. Pipe welders on rig-up, refinery turnaround, or pipeline work? $65-90/hr, often with per-diem. The reason: pipe welds are harder to make sound, harder to inspect, and welders who can pass X-ray on pipe are rare. This lesson is your ticket into that pay grade.

---

## Pipe positions — memorize these visually

| Position | Pipe orientation | Weld path | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1G / 1GR** | Horizontal (axis horizontal), **rotated during welding** | Welder holds gun at ~12 o'clock, pipe rotates past | Easiest |
| **2G** | Vertical (axis vertical), pipe fixed | Weld runs horizontally around pipe (like a belt) | Medium |
| **5G** | Horizontal (axis horizontal), **pipe fixed** | Welder walks around pipe, welds in every position from 12 to 6 o'clock | Hard |
| **6G** | 45° angle (axis at 45°), pipe fixed | Every position at every angle — the hardest common pipe test | Hardest |

**1GR "rolled" is the sweet spot for practice** — you rotate the pipe on rollers or by hand while welding at 12 o'clock. Once you have solid technique rolled, progress to fixed positions.

---

## Standard industrial procedures for mild steel pipe

Two common combinations in Alberta pipe work:[^1][^2]

### Procedure A: Pipeline / downhill root
Used for cross-country pipeline, refinery process piping where speed matters and X-ray is the acceptance criterion.

- **Root pass:** GMAW short-arc, downhill (12 to 6), 0.035" ER70S-6, 75/25 Ar/CO2, 120-150 A / 17-19 V
- **Hot pass:** FCAW-G, uphill (6 to 12), 0.045" E71T-1, 75/25 Ar/CO2, 180-200 A / 22-24 V
- **Fill/cap:** FCAW-G, uphill, same wire, 200-240 A / 24-27 V

**Why downhill root:** the small, controlled short-arc puddle plus gravity assist gives fast, consistent penetration. Traditional stick (SMAW) pipeliners used cellulose (E6010) downhill for exactly the same reason.

### Procedure B: Refinery / uphill throughout
Used for high-pressure, code-critical piping (ASME B31.1, B31.3).

- **Root pass:** GMAW-P (pulsed), uphill, 0.035" ER70S-6, 90/10 Ar/CO2, machine-programmed pulse parameters (varies by machine)
- **Hot / fill / cap:** FCAW-G uphill, 0.045" E71T-1, standard parameters

**GMAW-P root uphill** is slower than downhill short-arc but gives cleaner sidewall fusion. Preferred for pressure vessel–grade pipe in refineries.

## The 5G walk — the signature pipe welder skill

5G pipe (fixed horizontal) requires you to weld in **every possible position** as you walk around the pipe:[^1]

**On the top half (12 to 3 o'clock):** you're welding overhead-ish → reduce amperage, small puddle, faster travel
**Around 3 o'clock (3 side):** vertical up position, standard technique
**On the bottom half (3 to 6):** progressing into flat-ish position at 6 o'clock

Traditional 5G downhill:
- Start at 12 o'clock, weld to 6 o'clock on one side
- Move to the other side of the pipe, start at 12, weld to 6
- Repeat with each pass

Uphill 5G:
- Start at 6 o'clock, weld up to 12 on one side
- Other side, 6 to 12
- Slower but stronger welds

**A well-executed 5G root pass on pipe with wire-feed processes shows a smooth, uniform bead all the way around with no starts/stops visible.**

## Downhill root technique (GMAW short-arc)

The signature move of pipeline welders:[^1][^5]

- **Wire:** 0.035" ER70S-6 or ER70S-3 (cheaper, adequate for most)
- **Gas:** 75/25 Ar/CO2 (smoother) or 100% CO2 (deeper penetration but more spatter)
- **Amperage/Voltage:** 120-150 A / 17-19 V (true short-arc, sounds like frying bacon)
- **Travel direction:** DOWNHILL (12 to 6)
- **Gun angle:** 5-15° drag (backhand) — even downhill, you drag the arc behind
- **Speed:** fast — the small puddle plus gravity assist means you can outrun most puddle problems
- **Keyhole:** small keyhole through the root; you're feeding the wire into the leading edge as you descend

**Critical:** if the wire tries to burn back to the contact tip, your voltage is too low OR your CTWD is too short. Adjust before you ruin the joint.

---

## Uphill fill/cap technique (FCAW-G)

After root, switch guns to 0.045" FCAW-G and go UPHILL:[^6]

- **Wire:** 0.045" E71T-1
- **Gas:** 75/25 Ar/CO2
- **Amperage/Voltage:** 200-240 A fill / 200-220 A cap
- **Travel direction:** UPHILL (6 to 12)
- **Gun angle:** 5-15° drag angle
- **Weave:** slight side-to-side triangular weave, pause at toes
- **Speed:** slow, watch the puddle — E71T-1 slag freezes fast which lets you run all-position

## Joint prep for wire-feed on pipe

Standard prep for GMAW/FCAW pipe root:[^3][^4]

- **Bevel:** 30° per side = 60° included angle (some shops use 37.5° per side = 75° included for extra room)
- **Land / root face:** 1/16" to 3/32" (1.6-2.4 mm)
- **Root gap:** 1/8" (3.2 mm) for 0.035" GMAW root, tighter for smaller wire
- **Tack welds:** 3-4 tacks equally spaced (12, 3, 6, 9 o'clock on a 5G pipe), feathered/ground flush before starting

**Bevel prep is typically done with:**
- Beveling machine (dedicated pipe beveler — cleanest, fastest)
- Grinder (portable, done by hand — most common in field work)
- Oxyfuel cutting torch (rough cut, then grind smooth)

## Numbers you need to memorize

- **1GR = 1G rolled** — pipe axis horizontal, rotated during welding[^3]
- **2G pipe** — pipe axis VERTICAL, weld runs horizontally around pipe (like a belt)[^3]
- **5G pipe** — pipe axis HORIZONTAL, pipe FIXED, welder progresses around it[^3]
- **6G pipe** — pipe axis at 45°, pipe fixed, hardest common position[^3]
- **GMAW root pass wire (downhill pipeline):** 0.035" ER70S-6[^1]
- **FCAW pipe fill/cap:** 0.045" E71T-1[^6]
- **Shielding gas for pipe GMAW root:** 75/25 Ar/CO2 or 100% CO2[^2]
- **Shielding gas for FCAW-G pipe fill/cap:** 75/25 Ar/CO2 (E71T-1 preferred mix)[^6]
- **All wire-feed pipe polarity:** DCEP[^1][^6]
- **Standard bevel:** 30° per side = 60° included angle (or 37.5° per side = 75° for extra room)[^4]
- **Standard root gap:** 1/8" (3.2 mm) for 0.035" GMAW short-arc root[^1]
- **Standard land:** 1/16" to 3/32" (1.6-2.4 mm)[^1]
- **Downhill root progression:** 12 o'clock to 6 o'clock[^1]
- **Uphill fill/cap progression:** 6 o'clock to 12 o'clock[^6]
- **Tack welds on 5G pipe:** typically 3-4 equally spaced, feathered/ground flush before start[^4]
- **5G amperage adjustment at 12 (overhead-ish):** reduce 10-15% below flat setting[^1]

## What the textbook doesn't tell you

**Every pipe weld starts with fit-up.** Root openings that vary by 1/32" around the pipe cause welders to fail X-ray tests. A well-fit joint is 80% of a good weld. Take the extra time with clamps, wedges, and tack sequence to get a uniform 1/8" gap all the way around. Fit-up is a real skill.[^4]

**Tacks matter more than you think.** A poorly-placed tack becomes a discontinuity when you weld over it. Standard practice: 4 tacks at 12/3/6/9 on a 5G pipe, each tack ~1/2" long, feathered on both ends with a grinder BEFORE starting the root pass. The root pass ties into the feathered tack invisibly.[^4]

**Downhill root technique feels wrong at first.** Coming from plate welding where uphill is standard, letting gravity pull the puddle down feels like it should cause burn-through. It doesn't — the short-arc + small puddle actually gives you MORE control because gravity keeps the puddle small. Trust the process, practice on scrap first.[^1][^5]

**5G at 6 o'clock is where welders fail.** As you descend, you're now welding in the flat position but with the puddle wanting to sag. Slow down at the transition (~5 o'clock), reduce amperage slightly, and be careful not to let the puddle grow. Overheated 6 o'clock = drop-through and failed test.[^1]

**Watch your gas coverage in wind.** Field pipe welding often has wind. GMAW/FCAW-G shielding gas gets stripped in > 10 km/h wind. Options: wind wall/tarp, pop-up shelter, or switch to processes that don't need external gas (SMAW E6010/E7018, or FCAW-S with E71T-11). Miss this and you get porosity throughout the weld.[^6]

**Machine settings for pipe are different from plate.** Wire feed speed (WFS) for GMAW pipe root is typically 250-350 IPM (inches per minute) with 0.035" wire. For FCAW pipe fill: 300-450 IPM with 0.045" wire. Match your WFS to the amperage/voltage combination your machine's chart recommends.[^1][^2]

**Pipe travel around the joint should be SMOOTH.** Sudden speed changes or arc stops leave visible starts/stops. Practice walking around the pipe smoothly — footwork, body positioning, cable management. A pipe welder who can dance around a 5G joint without breaking stride runs beautiful welds.[^1]

**Radiographic testing (X-ray) shows every mistake.** The most common defects on wire-feed pipe are: incomplete penetration at the root, wagon-track porosity (usually contamination), incomplete fusion at bevel walls, and inclusions at start/stop points. Each has a specific fix — but the best fix is to run clean the first time.[^5]

## Key terms

- **1G / 1GR:** pipe horizontal, ROTATED (rolled) during welding — easiest pipe position
- **2G:** pipe vertical (axis vertical), weld horizontal around pipe
- **5G:** pipe horizontal, FIXED (welder walks around pipe)
- **6G:** pipe at 45°, fixed — hardest common position
- **Downhill:** weld progression from 12 o'clock to 6 o'clock (top to bottom)
- **Uphill:** weld progression from 6 o'clock to 12 o'clock (bottom to top)
- **1/2 stringer, 1/2 stringer pattern:** two-pass technique where one welder does one half, another does the other half
- **Walking the cup:** GTAW technique (not GMAW/FCAW) but often referenced in pipe welding culture
- **X-ray / RT (Radiographic Testing):** the standard acceptance test for critical pipe welds
- **Wagon-track porosity:** parallel lines of porosity on the sides of a weld (contamination indicator)

---

## Common exam trap

- **1GR = "1G rolled"** = horizontal pipe, ROTATED during welding. Not the same as 1G (fixed).
- **2G pipe** has pipe axis VERTICAL. Distractors may show pipe horizontal.
- **5G is FIXED** pipe (welder moves). 1G / 1GR is ROTATED pipe (pipe moves).
- **Downhill root pass on pipeline** is standard practice with GMAW short-arc. Distractors may say uphill is always required — that's for plate/refinery code work, not pipeline.
- **Bevel prep: 60° or 75° included** (30° or 37.5° per side). Not 45° included.
- **Tacks must be feathered before welding over them** to avoid discontinuity.
- **DCEP for all wire-feed pipe welding** with GMAW/FCAW-G/MCAW.
- **6G is the hardest common position** — every position at every angle. If you can pass 6G, you can weld any pipe joint.
- **Fit-up matters more than welding skill** — variable root gap = failed X-ray.

## Practice question preview

**Q:** A welder is preparing to run a 5G root pass on 6" schedule 40 mild steel pipe using GMAW with 0.035" ER70S-6. The joint is beveled to 60° included angle with a 1/16" land and 1/8" root gap. Four tack welds are in place at 12/3/6/9 o'clock positions. What should the welder do FIRST before striking the arc for the root pass?

A) Increase amperage 20% above normal 5G setting to burn through the tacks
B) Feather (grind smooth) both ends of each tack weld
C) Weld right over the tacks without modification
D) Remove all tacks and start from a fully open joint

**Correct: B**

**Explanation:** Tack welds must be feathered (ground smooth at both ends) before the root pass welds over them. Unmodified tacks become discontinuities where the root pass transitions from base metal to tack to base metal — creating an abrupt geometry change that causes lack of fusion, incomplete penetration, or wagon-track porosity at each tack location. A) Increasing amperage to burn through tacks causes over-penetration and root sag; C) Welding over unmodified tacks creates the exact defects that cause X-ray rejection; D) Removing tacks entirely leaves you without fit-up and the pipe will move as you weld.
