# GMAW, FCAW and MCAW Groove Welds on Mild Steel

Structural welding in Alberta is 80% wire-feed groove welds on mild steel. The industry-standard combination is **GMAW root pass, FCAW fill and cap**. It's fast, produces X-ray quality when done right, and every code shop from oil sands modules to bridge fabrication uses it. Master this and you're immediately employable as a fitter-welder in structural, tank, or fabrication work.

---

## Why combine GMAW and FCAW?

Each process is best at different parts of the joint:[^1][^2]

| Pass | Best process | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Root** | GMAW (short-arc or GMAW-P pulse) | Precise heat control, small puddle, easier to control open-root without burn-through |
| **Hot / fill / cap** | FCAW-G (E71T-1) or MCAW (E70C-6M) | High deposition rate, all-position with rutile flux, tolerant of tack welds and prior beads |

**A skilled welder switches wires mid-joint.** Root with 0.035" ER70S-6 on GMAW short-arc, then swap to 0.045" E71T-1 for fill and cap. Some shops have dual-torch cells; most just have two guns on two feeders.

---

## Groove weld anatomy — vocabulary you MUST know

Every joint has these features:[^2]

- **Bevel angle** — the angle of each cut face (measured from vertical). Typical: 22.5° per side = 45° included angle for open root; 30° per side = 60° included; 37.5° per side = 75° included.
- **Included angle** — total angle formed by both bevels (sum of both sides)
- **Root face / land** — the flat portion at the root of the bevel. Zero to 1/8" typical.
- **Root opening / gap** — the space between the two lands at the root. 0" for backing plate; 1/8"–3/16" for open root
- **Backing** — a piece of metal (permanent) or ceramic (removable) at the root to catch the first pass
- **Root pass** — the first bead at the bottom of the joint
- **Hot pass** — the second bead, typically at higher heat, that ties into the root
- **Fill passes** — subsequent beads that build up the joint
- **Cap / cover pass** — the final bead(s) that finish flush or slightly convex to the plate surface

---

## Positions on plate: 1G, 2G, 3G

For groove welds on plate:[^5]

| Position | Plate orientation | Weld axis | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1G** | Flat | Horizontal | Weld progresses along the seam with plate flat on the table |
| **2G** | Vertical | Horizontal | Weld runs horizontally across a vertical joint (imagine a horizontal seam on a standing sheet) |
| **3G** | Vertical | Vertical | Weld progresses vertically up (or down) the seam |
| **4G** | Overhead | Horizontal | Weld on the underside of a horizontal plate |

**Progression direction matters:**
- 3G up: standard for FCAW-G on structural (better puddle control, slag freezes fast)
- 3G down: fast but harder to get sidewall fusion; used on thinner material or open-root GMAW where speed matters

## Standard procedure: 3G open-root, GMAW root + FCAW fill/cap

This is the AIT/Red Seal signature procedure. Do it well and you have a fundamental structural qualification.

### Joint prep
- **Plate:** typically 3/8" or 1/2" mild steel
- **Bevel:** 30° per side (60° included)
- **Land:** 3/32" (2.4 mm)
- **Root gap:** 1/8" (3.2 mm) — matches 0.035" wire feed characteristics
- **Fit-up:** tack every 8-12", back-gouge and grind tacks flush before starting weld

### Root pass (GMAW short-arc, uphill)
- **Wire:** 0.035" ER70S-6[^3]
- **Gas:** 75/25 Ar/CO2 for smooth arc, low spatter — OR 100% CO2 for deeper penetration and looser slag[^3]
- **Polarity:** DCEP
- **Amperage/Voltage:** 130-160 A / 17-19 V (short-arc)
- **Travel:** UPHILL from bottom to top
- **Torch angle:** 5-10° drag angle (backhand)
- **Technique:** slight side-to-side sweep in the root, pausing at the toes to prevent undercut. Watch the keyhole — as the arc melts through, you'll see a small opening on the back side of the joint.
- **Speed:** slow enough to fill the root fully; too fast = incomplete fusion at the root

### Hot pass (FCAW-G, uphill)
- **Wire:** 0.045" E71T-1[^4]
- **Gas:** 75/25 Ar/CO2 (best for E71T-1) or 100% CO2 (cheaper, more spatter)
- **Polarity:** DCEP
- **Amperage/Voltage:** 180-220 A / 22-25 V
- **Travel:** UPHILL
- **Torch angle:** 5-15° drag angle
- **Technique:** slight side-to-side or vertical stringer over the root. Ties into and reinforces the root pass.
- **Between passes:** chip slag, wire brush, inspect. Never weld over dirty slag.

### Fill passes (FCAW-G, uphill)
- Same wire, gas, polarity
- **Amperage/Voltage:** 200-240 A / 24-27 V (higher than hot pass)
- **Technique:** side-to-side triangular weave OR series of stringer beads (welder's choice; stringers give better mechanical properties but take longer)
- Multiple passes until joint is filled just below the plate surface

### Cap pass (FCAW-G, uphill)
- Same wire, gas, polarity
- **Amperage/Voltage:** ~200 A / 23-25 V (slightly cooler than fill to avoid over-melting the toes)
- **Technique:** wider weave than fill passes; extend slightly onto base metal on both sides for good toe fusion
- **Finish:** slightly convex, flush with plate surface, or ground flush per specification

## 2G horizontal groove — critical technique differences

2G is the hardest position to master with wire-feed processes because the puddle wants to sag under gravity. Key techniques:[^1]

- **Multiple stringer beads (do NOT weave):** each bead is a narrow stringer running horizontally. Weaving fails because the puddle sags out of the upper portion.
- **Torch angle upward:** point the gun 5-15° above horizontal to counter gravity
- **Slower travel:** than 1G — you're depositing less per pass, so more passes needed
- **Pattern:** typical 6-8 stringer beads to fill a 30° bevel joint
- **Cap:** overlap stringer beads slightly like shingles on a roof

**FCAW-G is better than GMAW for 2G filling** because the rutile flux slag freezes fast and holds the puddle.

---

## 3G downhill — when to use it and when NOT to

3G downhill (top-to-bottom) is FAST but harder to get sidewall fusion. When it's OK:
- Thinner material (3/16" and under)
- Pipeline root passes (open-root, GMAW short-arc)
- Non-critical fabrication where speed matters and full penetration isn't critical

**When NOT to use it:**
- Any critical structural joint (bridges, pressure vessels, code work)
- Any position where full penetration is required
- Any joint where you'll be tested on radiographic soundness

**Downhill traps slag ahead of the puddle** if you're using FCAW-G. Stick to GMAW short-arc for downhill runs, and only where code allows.

---

## 4G overhead — the confidence builder

Overhead is intimidating but the puddle is small and manageable if you keep parameters cool:[^2]

- **Reduce amperage 15-20%** from flat setting
- **Very tight stringer beads** — puddle stays small, gravity works with you (surface tension holds a small puddle against the underside)
- **Fast travel** — no lingering in one spot
- **Multiple passes:** many small stringers rather than any weaving
- **Slag chipping is a pain:** wear a good FR jacket and expect showers of hot slag on every pass

---

## MCAW — when to choose it over FCAW

Metal-cored (MCAW) has advantages over flux-cored on clean, well-prepared joints:[^6]

| MCAW advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Higher deposition rate** | 10-15% more than E71T-1 at the same amperage |
| **Minimal slag** | Almost no chipping between passes; ideal for robotic welding |
| **Cleaner mechanical properties** | Fewer inclusions in critical applications |
| **Less spatter** | Cleaner welds in visible/architectural fab |

**MCAW disadvantages:**
- **Intolerant of dirty steel** — no flux to burn off contaminants
- **Requires clean joint prep** — grind mill scale off, wire brush, wipe with acetone
- **Requires shielding gas** — Ar/CO2 mixes (75/25 or 90/10 for better mechanicals)
- **More expensive wire** than FCAW

**Use MCAW when:** clean shop, robotic cells, architectural stainless-adjacent applications, high-volume production of clean parts.
**Use FCAW when:** field work, dirtier steel, all-position work, general structural fabrication.

## Numbers you need to memorize

- **GMAW root pass wire:** 0.035" ER70S-6 (typical)[^3]
- **FCAW fill/cap wire:** 0.045" E71T-1 (typical)[^4]
- **MCAW wire:** 0.045" E70C-6M (typical for clean steel)[^6]
- **Shielding gas for GMAW short-arc root:** 75/25 Ar/CO2 or 100% CO2[^3]
- **Shielding gas for FCAW-G fill/cap:** 75/25 Ar/CO2 or 100% CO2[^4]
- **Shielding gas for MCAW:** 75/25 or 90/10 Ar/CO2 (mixes preferred)[^6]
- **Polarity for all three:** DCEP[^3][^4][^6]
- **Standard bevel prep:** 30° per side = 60° included angle[^2]
- **Standard root gap:** 1/8" (3.2 mm)[^2]
- **Standard land:** 3/32" (2.4 mm)[^2]
- **GMAW root amperage (0.035"):** 130-160 A / 17-19 V (short-arc)[^1]
- **FCAW fill amperage (0.045"):** 200-240 A / 24-27 V[^1]
- **Torch angle:** 5-15° drag (backhand) for GMAW and FCAW[^1]
- **2G position:** stringer beads only, NO weaving[^1]
- **3G up:** better sidewall fusion, standard for code work[^5]
- **3G down:** faster but risks incomplete fusion; only for thin material or non-critical[^2]
- **Overhead amperage reduction:** 15-20% from flat[^2]
- **Vertical up amperage reduction:** 10-15% from flat[^1]

## What the textbook doesn't tell you

**Root pass is where welders get eliminated on qualification tests.** Get the root right and the rest is straightforward; miss the root and no amount of pretty fill/cap saves you when the coupon goes to the bend tester. Practice roots on scrap for weeks before your qualification.[^2]

**Between-pass cleaning is not optional.** Every pass, chip slag, wire brush, and inspect. Skipped chipping = slag inclusions in the next bead. Slag inclusions on X-ray = failed weld = failed qualification.[^5]

**"Whip and pause" for open-root GMAW.** Instead of continuous straight travel on the root, some welders use a whip motion (short forward push, brief pause) to control heat. Whipping lets the puddle solidify slightly between advances, giving better control over penetration. Optional but a real technique.[^2]

**Interpass temperature matters on multi-pass fills.** For most mild steel structural work, max interpass = 500 °F (260 °C). Too hot = large grain growth in HAZ, poor toughness. On a big joint, weld both sides alternately or take breaks to let the joint cool.[^5]

**FCAW-G loves cold days on jobsites.** Windy conditions can strip the shielding gas — either build a windbreak or switch to FCAW-S (E71T-11 self-shielded, DCEN). Never run FCAW-G in > 10 km/h wind without protection.[^4]

**Watch the smoke.** GMAW produces very little smoke. Excessive smoke on GMAW = arc problem (usually voltage too low). FCAW-G always produces significant smoke — that's the flux vaporizing. FCAW-S produces more smoke than -G. If you can't ventilate, wear a supplied-air respirator.[^4]

**Weave patterns for 3G up (personal preference):** triangle (zig-zag with pause at toes) is most common. Some welders prefer a "half-moon" (curved sweep). Both work. Whatever you use, PAUSE at the toes — that's where undercut hides.[^1]

**Cap-pass overlap should be minimal but visible.** Extend the cap 1/16" to 1/8" past each bevel edge onto the base metal. This ensures full fusion at the toes. Excess overlap = weld reinforcement too high, fails visual inspection.[^5]

## Key terms

- **Groove weld:** a weld made in a groove (V, U, J, or bevel-shaped) between two workpieces to achieve full penetration
- **Root pass / root bead:** the first weld pass at the bottom of the joint
- **Hot pass:** second pass that ties into the root, often at higher heat
- **Fill pass:** subsequent passes that build up the joint
- **Cap pass / cover pass:** the final pass at the top surface
- **Bevel angle:** the angle of each edge preparation (measured from vertical)
- **Included angle:** the total angle between both bevels
- **Root face / land:** the flat portion at the root of the joint
- **Root gap / opening:** the space between the two lands at the root
- **Backing:** metal or ceramic strip at the root to catch the first pass
- **Uphill / downhill:** direction of travel on vertical welds
- **Interpass temperature:** the temperature of the base metal between weld passes

---

## Common exam trap

- **2G horizontal = stringer beads only, NO weaving.** Weaving causes the upper portion of the puddle to sag out.
- **GMAW root + FCAW fill/cap** is the industry standard for structural work. Distractors will offer GMAW throughout or SMAW throughout.
- **3G downhill is FAST but risks lack of fusion.** Not used for critical/code work. 3G uphill is standard for structural.
- **Overhead: REDUCE amperage 15-20%.** Distractors will say increase.
- **DCEP for all three processes** (GMAW, FCAW-G, MCAW). DCEN only for FCAW-S (self-shielded).
- **Standard bevel prep for wire-feed groove:** 60° included angle (30° per side). Not 45°, not 75°.
- **Between-pass cleaning is mandatory** — chipping and wire-brushing prevents slag inclusions.
- **Interpass temperature limit** (~500 °F / 260 °C for mild steel) — exceeding hurts HAZ toughness.
- **Root gap typically 1/8" (3.2 mm)** for GMAW short-arc open root.

## Practice question preview

**Q:** A welder is qualifying on a 3G open-root groove test using GMAW root and FCAW-G fill/cap on 3/8" mild steel plate. Which sequence of parameter changes is correct progressing from root pass through cap pass?

A) Root: 0.045" FCAW at 250 A downhill; Fill: 0.035" GMAW at 100 A; Cap: SMAW at 150 A
B) Root: 0.035" GMAW short-arc at 140 A uphill; Fill: 0.045" FCAW at 220 A uphill; Cap: 0.045" FCAW at 200 A uphill
C) Root: FCAW-S at 300 A DCEN; Fill: GMAW short-arc at 90 A; Cap: SMAW at 120 A downhill
D) Root: GMAW spray at 280 A downhill; Fill: FCAW-S at 250 A; Cap: SMAW at 100 A

**Correct: B**

**Explanation:** The industry-standard 3G qualification procedure uses GMAW short-arc for the root pass (small puddle, precise control, uphill for penetration), then switches to FCAW-G at higher amperage for fill and cap. Fill passes run hotter than cap (cap reduced ~10-15% to avoid over-melting toes). A) FCAW downhill on the root risks slag inclusions and is not used for code root passes; C) FCAW-S is a field-work choice, not for a controlled shop qualification, and mixing processes randomly is wrong; D) GMAW spray-transfer downhill has severe fusion problems; SMAW cap after wire-feed is not a standard combination.
