# SMAW on Mild Steel Pipe — 2G, 5G, and Downhill Root

Pipe welding is where the Red Seal exam has the most questions and where the highest-paid journeypersons work. The 5G position — pipe fixed in horizontal position, welded without rotation — tests every skill simultaneously: flat, uphill vertical, overhead, and downhill, all in one joint. The welder who can produce X-ray quality 5G welds with E6010 root and E7018 fill/cap is ready for pipeline, pressure piping, and power generation work.

---

## Pipe Welding Vocabulary — The Essential Terms

Before touching a torch, you need to know the vocabulary. Pipe welding has its own language:[^3][^5]

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **OD** | Outside diameter — the dimension measured from outside edge to outside edge |
| **ID** | Inside diameter — OD minus 2× wall thickness |
| **Wall thickness** | The pipe wall, determined by schedule |
| **Root face / land** | The flat portion of the bevel at the root — critical for root gap control |
| **Root gap / root opening** | The space between the two pipe ends at the root — must be set precisely |
| **Root pass** | The first weld pass, fusing both root faces and bridging the root gap |
| **Hi-lo** | Misalignment at the joint — one pipe wall is higher than the other at the joint face |
| **Hot pass** | The second pass over the root — burns out any suck-back or incomplete root |
| **Suck-back** | Concavity at the root bead on the inside of the pipe — caused by too much heat on the root |
| **Tack weld** | A short temporary weld holding the joint in alignment before the root pass |
| **Feathering** | Grinding the ends of the tack welds to a thin, tapered edge so the root pass can fuse through them |
| **Clock positions** | Position on the pipe joint described by clock face — 12 o'clock = top, 6 o'clock = bottom, 3 o'clock = right, 9 o'clock = left |

## Pipe Positions — The Five Standard Positions

| Position | Pipe orientation | Pipe rotation | Welding zones |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1G** | Horizontal axis (pipe lying flat) | Pipe ROTATES — welder stays flat | All welding in flat zone (top of pipe) |
| **2G** | Vertical axis (pipe standing vertical) | Fixed — no rotation | Welder walks around pipe welding horizontally |
| **5G** | Horizontal axis (pipe lying flat) | Fixed — no rotation | Welder welds in all positions: overhead at 6 o'clock, uphill on sides, flat at top |
| **6G** | Inclined at 45° to horizontal | Fixed | All positions simultaneously — the hardest pipe test |

**For this section: 2G and 5G. GTAW 6G is covered in Section 5.**

## Joint Preparation and Fit-Up

### Pipe bevel preparation

Standard pipe groove for SMAW qualification:[^3]

- **Bevel angle:** 30° to 37.5° per side (included angle: 60° to 75°)
- **Root face (land):** 1/16" to 3/32" (1.6 to 2.4 mm) — factory bevels on pipe typically have 1/16" land
- **Root gap:** 3/32" to 1/8" (2.4 to 3.2 mm) — set to match the E6010 electrode diameter (3/32" is most common for 6G/5G qualification)

### Aligning and tacking

1. **Set root gap:** use a root gap gauge, a 3/32" rod end, or the electrode itself to set the gap. Consistent gap around the full circumference is critical.

2. **Check hi-lo:** with the gap set, check the pipe wall alignment. Maximum hi-lo typically 1.6 mm (1/16") per applicable code.[^2]

3. **Tack weld locations:** for 6" pipe, tack at 4 clock positions — 12 o'clock, 3 o'clock, 6 o'clock, and 9 o'clock (or at equal quarters). Tack welds with E6010 at same polarity/amperage as root pass.

4. **Tack weld length:** 25–40 mm (1" to 1-1/2") long tacks. Long enough to hold alignment; short enough to fuse through during the root pass.

### Feathering the tack welds

**Feathering is mandatory before root pass welding.** The ends of each tack weld must be ground (with an angle grinder and grinding disc or a die grinder with carbide burr) to a thin, tapered ("feathered") edge:[^3]

- **Why feather:** the root pass must fuse through the tack weld starts and stops. A tack with a blunt end creates a cold lap or lack of fusion at the transition.
- **How:** grind the start and stop ends of each tack to a gradual, thin, flame-like taper — like a feather tip
- **Result:** when the root pass arc approaches the tack weld, it can melt through the thin tapered end gradually, without the abrupt step of a blunt tack end

## Root Pass Technique — E6010

The E6010 root pass on pipe is the most technique-critical weld in the trade. Everything that follows depends on it.[^3][^5]

### Parameters

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| **Electrode** | E6010, 3/32" (2.4 mm) |
| **Amperage** | 65–85 A (set at lower end for overhead portions) |
| **Polarity** | DCEP |
| **Arc length** | Very short — barely one electrode diameter |

### 5G Position — Working Around the Clock

The 5G is a fixed horizontal pipe. The welder must weld uphill on the sides and overhead at the bottom. The standard technique is to divide the pipe into two halves and weld each half separately:[^3]

**Half-pipe welding technique (two-half method):**

**First half — from 6 o'clock uphill to 12 o'clock (right side):**
1. Start at 6 o'clock (overhead) — strike arc, establish keyhole
2. Travel uphill (counterclockwise when viewed from the leading end) to 12 o'clock
3. Stop at 12 o'clock — taper the weld out slowly before stopping

**Second half — from 6 o'clock uphill to 12 o'clock (left side):**
1. Resume at 6 o'clock (clean and grind the start of the first half slightly)
2. Travel uphill (clockwise) to 12 o'clock
3. Tie in to the first half at 12 o'clock — "tie-in" at the top

**Why this approach:** welding uphill in both directions provides better penetration control and avoids the difficulty of welding downhill on a round pipe with an open root.

### Starting at 6 o'clock (overhead)

The overhead start at the bottom of the 5G pipe is the hardest moment in the root pass:[^5]

- Body position: squat or sit under the pipe — find a stable position you can hold for 30–60 seconds
- Strike the arc slightly before 6 o'clock — start on the tack weld at 6 o'clock, then move back to the 6 o'clock start position
- Short arc, low amperage — the puddle must be small enough for surface tension to hold it
- Establish the keyhole before moving — wait until you can see through to the other side

### Keyhole technique for open root

The keyhole is the small opening at the leading edge of the root pass weld pool:[^3]

- **Correct keyhole size:** approximately 1–1.5× the root gap width
- **Too small keyhole:** incomplete penetration — the root isn't fusing through
- **Too large keyhole:** burn-through — the root is a hole, not a weld
- **Watch the keyhole constantly:** travel speed controls keyhole size. Slower = larger keyhole. Faster = smaller keyhole.
- **Listen to the arc:** a consistent, steady arc sound = correct arc length. Crackling, sputtering = arc too long (reduce by moving torch in).

### Welding over tack welds

When you approach a tack weld during the root pass:[^3]
1. Slow down slightly before the tack — let the puddle fill up to the feathered edge
2. Maintain the keyhole through the tack
3. Speed up slightly over the thick part of the tack — the tack absorbs heat quickly
4. Slow back down as you exit the tack on the other side
5. Re-establish the keyhole after the tack before resuming normal speed

## 5G Downhill Root Pass

5G downhill (starting at 12 o'clock and welding down to 6 o'clock) is specifically required by the AIT curriculum. Downhill pipe root is the standard technique for **cross-country pipeline welding** (API 1104).[^3]

### When downhill is used

- **Cross-country pipeline:** cellulosic electrodes (E6010/E6011) downhill — faster, practical for long-seam pipeline welds in the field
- **Structural piping (ASME B31.3 shop/field):** uphill is required or preferred — downhill is generally not allowed on code pressure piping

### Downhill root technique

| Variable | Downhill root (5G) |
|---|---|
| **Electrode** | E6010, 3/32" (2.4 mm) |
| **Amperage** | 70–90 A (slightly higher than uphill — faster travel requires more heat) |
| **Travel direction** | Starting at 12 o'clock, moving toward 6 o'clock |
| **Torch angle** | 5–15° in the direction of travel (pushing the puddle downward slightly) |

**Key difference from uphill:** in downhill root welding, the puddle tries to run ahead of the arc (gravity pulls liquid downward). The technique requires faster travel to keep ahead of the running puddle, and the arc must stay at the leading edge of the puddle — not in the puddle itself.

**Downhill defect risk:** downhill root passes are susceptible to:
- **Slag running ahead of the arc** (particularly bad for fill passes with slow-freeze slags)
- **Cold lap** if travel is too slow (puddle piles up over the root gap without fusing through)
- **Lack of fusion at toes** because the fast travel may not let the arc dwell long enough at the sidewall

**Downhill fill and cap passes with E7018:** typically NOT done — E7018 produces a slow-freezing slag that runs easily downhill, trapping slag in the weld. Downhill fill/cap is only used with specific electrodes (cellulosic or fast-freeze types). Verify against the WPS.

## Hot Pass — After the Root

The hot pass is the second pass, run at higher amperage than the root, specifically to:[^3]

- Burn out any suck-back (concavity) or cold spots in the root bead
- Establish a good base for fill passes
- Remove any slag trapped in the root bead surface

**Hot pass parameters:**
- **Electrode:** E6010, 1/8" (3.2 mm)
- **Amperage:** 90–110 A — higher than root pass to generate heat quickly
- **Speed:** faster than root pass — just enough heat to fuse, not enough to burn through

## Fill and Cap — E7018

### Fill passes (5G — multiple position zones)

The fill passes in 5G require adjusting technique continuously as the welder moves through different positions around the pipe:[^4]

| Clock position | Technique |
|---|---|
| **6 o'clock (overhead)** | Very tight stringer, lowest amperage — same as overhead plate |
| **3-4 o'clock or 8-9 o'clock (rising side, transitioning to vertical)** | Slight weave begins, amperage increases |
| **3 or 9 o'clock (true vertical)** | Uphill technique — weave with pause at toes, moderate amperage |
| **12 o'clock (flat top)** | Largest weave or stringer, highest amperage — similar to flat plate |

### Typical E7018 parameters (5G fill)

- **Electrode:** 1/8" (3.2 mm)
- **Overhead zone amperage:** 90–110 A
- **Flat zone amperage:** 120–145 A
- **Between passes:** chip and wire brush every pass — slag must be completely clean before the next bead

### Cap pass

- Slightly reduced amperage from fill — caps are susceptible to undercut if too hot
- Stringer or weave depending on joint width
- Extend 1/16" onto base metal at each toe
- Final check with a mirror and flashlight at the overhead zone — undercut at 6 o'clock is common and easy to miss

## What the textbook doesn't tell you

**The start and stop at 12 o'clock is where the test is won or lost.** Both halves of the 5G root join at the top. A poor tie-in — where the two root passes meet — produces a crown bump, a cold area, or a trapped inclusion. Practice the tie-in as a separate skill. The technique: approach the existing tie-in end, overlap by 10–15 mm, and taper your stop to match the shape of the existing weld. Then restart the second half going the other direction, tying into that same point.[^3]

**The overhead zone in 5G is physically exhausting on a full-size pipe.** Unlike the overhead plate test (a few hundred millimetres), the overhead zone on a 6" pipe is approximately 180° of circumference = about 260 mm of overhead welding per half. Positioning, torch control, and physical endurance must all be high simultaneously. Practice the full sequence, not just the overhead section in isolation.[^5]

**Downhill root with E6010 sounds different from uphill.** The forceful, driving downhill arc has a slightly faster, more aggressive sound than the uphill arc. The keyhole in downhill is harder to see because the puddle flows toward you rather than away. Some welders find it helpful to look for the keyhole "flash" rather than the keyhole shape when welding downhill at the 12 o'clock position.[^3]

**Amperage changes around the clock are real and needed.** Some welders set one amperage for the whole pipe. This produces acceptable results on some passes but will cause undercut or drips at the overhead zone and excessive buildup at the flat zone. Carry a remote amperage control or learn where your machine's amperage is and set it before each zone.[^5]

## Key terms

- **5G:** fixed horizontal pipe, no rotation — the welder moves through flat, vertical, and overhead positions
- **2G:** fixed vertical pipe, no rotation — welder welds horizontally around the circumference
- **Root pass:** the first weld pass, fusing both root faces and bridging the root gap
- **Hot pass:** the second pass over the root at higher heat — eliminates suck-back and cold spots
- **Suck-back:** concavity of the root bead inside the pipe — caused by excessive heat or too slow travel
- **Keyhole:** the small through-opening at the leading edge of the root pass puddle — the indicator of proper penetration
- **Hi-lo (misalignment):** the difference in pipe wall height at the joint face — maximum 1.6 mm for code pipe
- **Feathering:** grinding tack weld ends to a thin taper so the root pass can fuse through smoothly
- **Tie-in:** the point where the two halves of a root pass meet at 12 o'clock — requires precise technique
- **Downhill:** welding direction from the top of the pipe (12 o'clock) to the bottom (6 o'clock) — used in pipeline work
- **Clock positions:** the description of location on the pipe joint by analogy to a clock face

---

## Common exam trap

- **5G = fixed horizontal pipe; 6G = inclined pipe.** The numbers are frequently confused. 5G does NOT rotate. 1G does rotate.
- **E6010 root pass at 6 o'clock (overhead in 5G) requires LOWER amperage** than the flat zone at 12 o'clock. The exam may ask for the correct adjustment direction — always reduce for overhead.
- **Feathering is ALWAYS required on tack welds before root pass.** It is not optional. Missing feathering creates lack of fusion at the tack-to-root-pass transition.
- **Downhill root pass is used in PIPELINE welding (API 1104)** — NOT in standard ASME B31.3 pressure piping. The code context matters.
- **Hi-lo maximum is 1.6 mm (1/16")** — not 3 mm, not 1/8". This is a frequent code-specific number on the exam.
- **The 6G pipe qualification (see Section 5) covers all pipe AND plate positions.** The 5G qualification covers: 1G, 2G, 5G pipe — NOT 6G, and not all plate positions.

## Practice question preview

**Q:** A welder is welding the root pass in the 5G position on 6" schedule 80 pipe using E6010, 3/32" diameter. When approaching a tack weld at the 3 o'clock position, which action best describes the correct technique?

A) Stop and grind out the tack weld completely before continuing  
B) Speed up to pass through the tack quickly, then return to normal speed  
C) Slow down slightly before the feathered tack, maintain the keyhole through the tack, then speed up slightly over the thickest part before resuming normal speed  
D) Increase amperage by 20 A to ensure the root pass fuses through the thicker tack weld material

**Correct: C**

**Explanation:** Feathered tack welds should not be ground out — they are part of the root pass system and fusing through them correctly is a required skill. The correct technique at a feathered tack: approach slowly (the thin feathered end requires less heat), maintain the keyhole to ensure through-fusion, and speed up slightly over the thicker central portion of the tack (which absorbs heat faster), then return to normal speed once past the other feathered end. Option B (speed up) risks cold lapping at the entry to the tack. Option D (increase amperage) risks burn-through through the tack. Option A (grind out) is incorrect — the tack is placed to maintain alignment and remains in the joint.
