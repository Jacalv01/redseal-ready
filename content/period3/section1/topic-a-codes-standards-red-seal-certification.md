# Codes, Standards and Red Seal Certification

The difference between a journeyperson and a journeyperson who works in code shops is this: the journeyperson who works in code shops understands WHY the welds have to be done a certain way. Codes are not bureaucratic paperwork — they are the accumulated engineering judgement of what it takes to keep pressure vessels from exploding, bridges from collapsing, and pipelines from rupturing. When you understand the code system, you can work in any sector of the trade.

---

## Code, Standard, Specification — Three Different Things

These three words are used interchangeably in the field, but they mean different things to engineers:[^1][^2]

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Code** | A legally enforceable set of requirements adopted by a jurisdiction (government body). Backed by law. | CSA B51 (provincial law in AB), ASME BPVC (adopted by AB Safety Codes Act) |
| **Standard** | A document produced by a consensus body (CSA, AWS, ASTM, ASME) that defines requirements. It becomes a code when adopted into law. | CSA W47.1, CSA W59, AWS D1.1 |
| **Specification** | A document that defines requirements for a specific material, product, or process in a specific project or contract. | A WPS (Welding Procedure Specification) for a specific pipe weld |

**Why it matters on the job:** When your foreman says "weld it to code," they mean the adopted standard applies. When they hand you a WPS, they're giving you a project-level specification. Both are binding.

## Who Sets the Standards? The Agencies

Each agency has a domain. Know which one owns which territory:[^1][^2][^3]

| Agency | Full Name | Main Domain |
|---|---|---|
| **CSA** | Canadian Standards Association | Canadian welding standards (W47.1, W59, B51, W117.2, W178.1) |
| **ASME** | American Society of Mechanical Engineers | Pressure vessels and pressure piping (BPVC, B31.3) |
| **AWS** | American Welding Society | Filler metal classifications (A5.x series), structural (D1.1), pipe (D1.1 ch. 9) |
| **API** | American Petroleum Institute | Petroleum pipeline (API 1104) and oil field equipment |
| **NBBI** | National Board of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Inspectors | Inspection, repair, and re-rating of pressure vessels after initial code construction |
| **CGSB** | Canadian General Standards Board | NDT personnel certification (CGSB 48.9712) |
| **ASTM** | American Society for Testing and Materials | Material test methods (E8 tensile, E23 Charpy), material classifications (A36, A516 Gr. 70) |

## The Major Welding Codes — What They Govern

### CSA W47.1 — Company Certification for Welding[^1]

This is THE most important code for Alberta welders to understand. **CSA W47.1 certifies the COMPANY**, not the welder.

- **What it requires:**
  - A Certified Welding Engineer (CWE) or Engineer of Record must approve all WPS documents
  - The company must maintain qualified welders (tested to the WPS)
  - Quality control records must be kept
  - Third-party audits by CWB Group maintain certification
- **Who needs it:** Any company doing structural welding, pressure piping, or fabrication that will be inspected by a Safety Codes Officer in Alberta
- **Division structure:** Division 1 (complete certification), Division 2 (limited scope), Division 3 (on-site repair and maintenance)
- **As a welder:** When you work for a W47.1 company, your welder qualification cards are issued under that company's certification. If you leave, you may need to requalify at the new company — unless the new company accepts the same WPS scope.

### CSA W59 — Welded Steel Construction[^2]

Governs how steel structures (buildings, bridges, towers) are welded.

- **What it covers:**
  - Design of welded connections (throat size calculations, load types)
  - Workmanship (acceptable defect limits, interpass temperature, preheat by carbon equivalent)
  - Welder qualification positions and test requirements
  - Inspection requirements (VT, PT, MT, UT, RT criteria)
- **Key clauses welders use:**
  - Clause 4 (General requirements, preheat calculation by CE)
  - Clause 12 (Welder qualification — what positions qualify you for what)
  - Annex A (filler metal prequalification)
- **As a welder:** Your 3G plate test may qualify you for 1G and 3G production welds. W59 Clause 12 defines the qualification matrix exactly.

### CSA B51 — Boiler, Pressure Vessel and Pressure Piping Code[^1]

The provincial law that regulates all pressure-containing equipment in Alberta. It adopts ASME BPVC by reference.

- **Why it matters:** If the vessel holds compressed fluid — steam, process gas, hydraulic — B51 applies. Alberta's Safety Codes Act enforces B51.
- **As a welder:** You'll be welding to ASME BPVC Section IX (procedure and performance qualification), but the authority is B51. Your qualification documents must be traceable.

### ASME BPVC Section IX — Welding Qualifications[^3]

The most detailed qualification code in the industry. Used for pressure vessels, pressure piping, and anything adopted by ASME.

- **Part QW:** Welding (by far the most relevant part)
  - **QW-100 through QW-199:** General requirements and definitions
  - **QW-200 through QW-299:** Procedure qualification (PQR)
  - **QW-300 through QW-399:** Performance qualification (welder qualification)
  - **QW-400 through QW-499:** Essential and supplementary essential variables
  - **QW-500 through QW-599:** Filler metal classification and test requirements

### API 1104 — Welding of Pipelines[^2]

Used for cross-country petroleum pipeline welds.

- **Key difference from ASME:** API 1104 uses acceptance criteria based on radiographic testing of production welds (not just qualification coupons)
- **Pipeline root pass:** API 1104 allows and commonly uses E6010 downhill stringer bead root pass (unique to pipeline work)
- **As a welder:** Pipeline work typically requires you to qualify on API 1104 separately from your ASME or W47.1 cards

## Welding Procedure Specification (WPS) and Procedure Qualification Record (PQR)

This is the heart of code welding. Every code weld must be backed by a documented procedure.[^1][^3]

### The three-document chain

```
PQR (test results) → supports → WPS (the instructions) → referenced by → Work Traveller/Blueprint (the job)
```

### WPS — Welding Procedure Specification

A WPS is a written document that instructs the welder how to make a specific weld. It must include:[^3]

| WPS element | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Base metal** | P-Number, Group Number, thickness range |
| **Filler metal** | F-Number, A-Number, AWS classification, diameter range |
| **Welding process** | SMAW, GTAW, FCAW, etc. |
| **Positions** | 1G, 2G, 3G, 4G, 5G, 6G |
| **Preheat and interpass temperature** | Min preheat °C, max interpass °C |
| **PWHT** | Temperature and time range (if required) |
| **Shielding gas** | Composition, flow rate range |
| **Electrical parameters** | Amperage, voltage, travel speed, polarity |
| **Joint design** | Bevel angle, root opening, backing |
| **Technique** | Stringer or weave, uphill or downhill |

**The welder FOLLOWS the WPS.** Deviating from the WPS — even if your instinct says otherwise — is a code violation. If the WPS is wrong, report it to the CWE. Don't change parameters on your own.

### PQR — Procedure Qualification Record

A PQR is the test record that proves the WPS parameters actually produce sound welds. It documents:[^3]

- The actual measured parameters during the test weld (amperage, voltage, travel speed)
- The test results (bend test, tensile test, radiographic examination)
- Signature of the testing agency or CWE who witnessed it

**The PQR certifies the procedure. The WPS certifies the instructions.** They must be consistent.

## Essential Variables vs Non-Essential Variables

When any essential variable changes, you need a new PQR and WPS (or a re-qualification test). Non-essential variables can be changed by amending the WPS without re-testing.[^3]

### Essential variables for welder performance qualification (ASME Section IX)[^3]

| Change | Essential? |
|---|---|
| Process change (SMAW → GTAW) | YES — new test required |
| Position change (1G → 3G) | YES (going to harder position requires test in that position) |
| Filler metal F-Number change | YES |
| Backing removal (with backing → open root) | YES |
| Polarity change (DCEP → DCEN) | YES |
| Electrical characteristic (AC → DC) | YES |

### Non-essential variables (can change without re-test)

| Change | Non-essential? |
|---|---|
| Amperage within qualified range | YES |
| Travel speed adjustment within range | YES |
| Joint design changes | YES (within prequalified limits) |
| Pass sequence (stringer vs weave) | YES |
| Preheat temperature increase | YES (decrease may be essential) |

**Exam trap:** Adding backing (going from open root to backed joint) is generally NOT essential for a welder qualification — it makes the joint EASIER. Removing backing IS essential — it makes the joint harder.

## Welder Performance Qualification

When you do a qualification test, here's what you're proving — and what it buys you:[^1][^3]

### Qualification test coupon → production positions covered

Under ASME Section IX QW-303 and CSA W47.1 Annex B:[^1][^3]

| Test coupon | Production positions qualified |
|---|---|
| 1G plate (flat) | 1G only |
| 2G plate (horizontal) | 1G, 2G |
| 3G plate (vertical up) | 1G, 3G |
| 4G plate (overhead) | 1G, 4G |
| 3G + 4G plate combination | 1G, 2G, 3G, 4G (all positions on plate) |
| 6G pipe (inclined, rotated) | All pipe positions (2G, 5G, 6G) AND all plate positions |

**The 6G is the gold standard qualification.** It qualifies you for everything. Pipeline companies and pressure vessel shops often require a 6G for employment.

### How long does a qualification last?

Under ASME Section IX QW-322:[^3]

- Qualification is valid as long as you weld in that process at least **once every 6 months**
- If you don't weld in the process for 6+ months, your qualification expires and you must re-test
- Medical incapacitation with supervisor documentation may extend this

## Carbon Equivalent and Preheat — Code Requirement

CSA W59 Annex A requires preheat based on the carbon equivalent (CE) of the steel.[^2]

### Carbon equivalent formula (IIW method)

**CE = C + Mn/6 + (Cr + Mo + V)/5 + (Ni + Cu)/15**[^2]

Where all values are weight % from the mill test report.

| CE Range | Preheat required (CSA W59) |
|---|---|
| CE < 0.35 | None required (for t < 25 mm) |
| CE 0.35 – 0.55 | Min preheat 93 °C (200 °F) |
| CE > 0.55 | Min preheat 150 °C (300 °F) or per engineering |

**Read the mill test report (MTR) before you strike an arc.** The CE is calculated from the chemistry shown on the MTR. If CE > 0.35 and you don't preheat, you risk hydrogen cracking in the HAZ — and the weld fails inspection.

## Using the RSOS to Prepare for Red Seal

The Red Seal Occupational Standard (RSOS) is your exam blueprint.[^5]

### RSOS structure

```
Blocks (MWA) → Tasks → Sub-tasks → Performance Criteria
```

- **Block A** (Common Occupational Skills) — 16% of exam, 20 questions
- **Block B** (Layout and Fabrication) — 22% of exam, 28 questions
- **Block C** (Cutting and Gouging) — 18% of exam, 23 questions
- **Block D** (Welding Processes) — 44% of exam, 54 questions

### How to use the RSOS for studying

1. **Download the RSOS PDF** from red-seal.ca — it's free
2. Every sub-task (e.g., D-13.04 "Performs weld using SMAW equipment") lists performance criteria that exam questions are written from
3. For each sub-task, ask yourself: "Could someone write a multiple-choice question about this criterion? What would the wrong answers look like?"
4. Pay attention to the **"common core" indicator** — common core sub-tasks are tested in every jurisdiction. Alberta-specific tasks are also tested but weighted differently in some jurisdictions.

**The Red Seal exam has 125 questions. You need 88 correct (70%) to pass.** Block D (Welding Processes) has 54 questions — if you're weak there, fix it first.

## Numbers you need to memorize

- **Red Seal exam:** 125 questions, 4 hours, 70% pass mark (88 correct)[^5]
- **Period exam pass mark:** 70%[^5]
- **ASME Section IX minimum test coupon thickness:** 3/8" (9.5 mm) for groove welds unless using thinner parent material[^3]
- **Welder qualification expiry:** 6 months without process use, per ASME QW-322[^3]
- **CE < 0.35:** No preheat required for t < 25 mm (W59)[^2]
- **CE 0.35–0.55:** Min preheat 93 °C (200 °F)[^2]
- **CE > 0.55:** Min preheat 150 °C (300 °F)[^2]
- **6G pipe qualification:** qualifies all pipe AND plate positions[^1][^3]
- **3G + 4G plate combination:** qualifies all plate positions[^1]

## What the textbook doesn't tell you

**Your qualification cards are portable, but your WPS coverage isn't.** The card says you passed a 3G test. The WPS says the joint geometry, filler metal, and material limits. When you move to a new company, check that their WPS scope matches what you've qualified on — you may need a new test coupon on their materials.[^1]

**Pipeline companies run their own qualification tests regardless of your existing cards.** Even if you have a 6G ASME card, a pipeline company will make you run a test coupon on their pipe, to their WPS, witnessed by their welding inspector. This is normal and expected.[^2]

**The WPS is a living document.** Engineering changes, base metal substitutions, filler metal changes — all require WPS revisions and PQR re-confirmation. If you're handed a WPS dated 2015 and the job is using 2024 material grades, ask if the WPS has been updated.[^3]

**"Prequalified" WPS (CSA W47.1 Annex D / AWS D1.1 Annex B):** Some joints are so standard that they don't require a PQR test — the code prequalifies them. Conditions are strict (specific filler metals, joint geometry, process combinations). Your company may use prequalified WPS for standard structural work, saving the cost of test coupons.[^1]

**The CGSB NDT certification is separate from your welder qualification.** The person interpreting your radiographs, doing your UT, or reading your PT is a CGSB-certified inspector. As a welder, you should understand what they're looking for — it makes you a better first-pass inspector of your own work.[^4]

## Key terms

- **Code:** A legally enforceable set of requirements adopted by a jurisdiction
- **Standard:** A consensus document that defines requirements; becomes a code when adopted by law
- **Specification:** A project-level document defining requirements for a specific item
- **WPS (Welding Procedure Specification):** Written instructions for making a specific weld
- **PQR (Procedure Qualification Record):** Test record that validates the WPS parameters
- **Essential variable:** A parameter change that requires a new PQR test
- **Non-essential variable:** A parameter change that requires only a WPS amendment
- **Carbon equivalent (CE):** A formula relating steel chemistry to hardenability and preheat requirement
- **6G:** The inclined rotated pipe position — the highest qualification, covers all positions
- **CWE:** Certified Welding Engineer — must approve WPS documents under W47.1
- **CWB Group:** Canadian Welding Bureau — third-party auditor for W47.1 certification
- **MAWP:** Maximum Allowable Working Pressure — a design parameter on pressure vessels
- **MTR / MTC:** Mill Test Report / Mill Test Certificate — the chemistry and mechanical properties of the plate/pipe as delivered from the steel mill

---

## Common exam trap

- **"Code" and "standard" are NOT the same thing.** A code is law. A standard is adopted into law to become a code. Exam questions will try to reverse these definitions.
- **CSA W47.1 certifies the COMPANY, not the individual welder.** Questions may ask "what does W47.1 certify?" — the answer is the company's welding operations, not the welder personally.
- **Adding backing = easier test (NOT essential variable).** Removing backing = harder test (IS essential). Don't flip these.
- **The 3G coupon alone qualifies you for 1G and 3G only — NOT 2G or 4G.** Need 3G + 4G (or 2G + 3G + 4G) for full plate coverage.
- **Welder qualification expires after 6 months without process use** — not 3 months, not 1 year.
- **ASME Section IX, CSA W47.1, and AWS D1.1 have DIFFERENT qualification matrices.** A weld position that qualifies you for more positions under one code may be more restrictive under another. Always check which code applies.
- **CE formula:** C + Mn/6 + (Cr+Mo+V)/5 + (Ni+Cu)/15 — memorize the denominators (6, 5, 15). They will put 3, 5, 6 in the wrong places.

## Practice question preview

**Q:** A welder has a valid ASME Section IX 3G plate qualification but has not performed any SMAW welding for 8 months. Which of the following best describes the welder's qualification status?

A) Still valid — plate qualifications do not expire  
B) Expired — the welder must re-test on a 3G coupon  
C) Suspended — the welder must submit documentation to ASME  
D) Valid for 6 more months after submitting an extension request

**Correct: B**

**Explanation:** ASME Section IX QW-322 states that a welder's qualification expires if they have not welded with that process in any 6-month period. 8 months exceeds this limit. The welder must run a new test coupon to re-establish qualification. There is no "suspension" status or extension request mechanism in QW-322 — the qualification simply lapses and requires re-testing.

**Red Seal mapping:** A-4.01 (Uses documentation and reference material)
